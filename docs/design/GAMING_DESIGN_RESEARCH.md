# Nivaro Gaming — design research and review brief

Date: 2026-10-27, Asia/Tehran. Status: Blue RGB dark/light baseline accepted for implementation; research/reference evidence retained; no application implementation.

The owner authorized gaming-store research, static visual proposals, and necessary documentation changes, selected independent USD pricing/sales, accepted the Blue RGB theme pair, and delegated finalization of the three assembly contract gaps. The canonical requirements remain in [NIVARO_ROADMAP_EN.md](../NIVARO_ROADMAP_EN.md), revision 1.3.0. The previous Persian reading copy is not synchronized.

## Decision in one paragraph

Use the working display brand **Nivaro Gaming** with a product-led hardware showroom and a practical assembly workspace. Make components, build-your-own, ready systems, equipment and comparison immediately discoverable. Following owner feedback, blue is the primary brand spectrum on black/dark and white/light surfaces. Preserve clearly multicolor RGB lighting, including red, purple, indigo, green and other hues, in product imagery and restrained environmental accents. Keep controls and technical content readable. Keep the current folder/repository identity: a rename is optional and not needed to establish the brand. No domain or trademark availability is claimed.

### Owner feedback — blue identity with multicolor RGB

The owner correctly noted that four images were shown: three home concepts and one workflow board. The prior numbering was ambiguous. Future review should use descriptive image labels rather than making the workflow board look like an unnumbered alternative.

The green-dominant Gear Gallery palette is rejected. Green remains welcome as one color in RGB lighting and as a semantic status color; it must not dominate the logo, primary actions or overall theme. The approved **Blue RGB — dark** and **Blue RGB — light** pair became the visual implementation baseline. Both are required, complementary theme states. Approval of the direction does not establish exact product imagery, accessible contrast, functioning motion or responsive behavior; verify these during implementation.

### Conclusions from the live references

- Hardware shops benefit from a broad, recognizable taxonomy, but stacking every promotion and carousel makes the main task harder to identify. Nivaro's home should give assembly one primary action and keep deeper equipment discovery below it.
- Teccaf demonstrates a practical component-picker workflow. Nivaro should improve the empty/incomplete semantics: show no selected subtotal as a dash, a partial subtotal as partial, and compatibility uncertainty explicitly. Selecting a component is not a completed order.
- Razer makes edition, assembly state and layout distinct purchase choices. For keyboards, RGB is only one attribute; size, switch type, hot-swap support, connectivity and layout should remain usable filters.
- GSI's visible themes support exploring a deliberately designed light mode. A dark hero is not a reason to sacrifice readability in filters, comparison tables or checkout.
- Dragon Shop's rotating promotions and Razer's manually switched gallery are different motion uses. Our proposal uses a stable hero and user-controlled product galleries; small feedback animations are specified separately below.

## Visual system proposal

The signature is a **build workbench**, not generic neon decoration: a well-lit PC and selected components, then a clear path into assembly and comparison. Spend visual emphasis on hardware photography and the main action. Keep price/specification surfaces quiet.

| Role | Dark | Light |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page | Near-black `#080A10` | White `#FFFFFF` with restrained off-white section separation |
| Raised surface | Neutral charcoal `#121722` | `#F7F9FC` or white |
| Main text | `#EDF3FA` | Ink `#172235` |
| Primary action | Royal/electric blue `#2563EB` with white text | The same blue family `#2563EB` with white text |
| Blue hierarchy | Lighter blue `#65A7FF` for emphasis; indigo in subtle lighting | Darker blue text and blue actions; independently measured contrast |
| RGB decoration | Clearly multicolor red, magenta, purple, indigo, blue, cyan, green and limited warm accents in hardware lighting and localized reflections | The same spectrum in hardware lighting and restrained low-opacity reflections on white |
| Status | Separate success/warning/error tokens plus text/icon | Same meanings; actual contrast checked independently |

These exact hex values are proposed design tokens, not user-approved final values or measured screenshot colors. RGB is decorative and must not obscure text or become a status signal by itself. Avoid rainbow body text and pervasive glowing card borders. A change of theme preserves content hierarchy, choices and market rather than changing the product experience.

Typography: Persian surfaces use Vazirmatn with Inter for Latin model names; English surfaces may use Space Grotesk for display and Inter for body/controls. Use at most two font families on a given locale's screen. Typeface licenses, weights and package versions must be verified before installation. These are proposals, not installed dependencies. Use tabular numerals for prices, bidi isolation for Latin model names, and ordinary readable type in checkout/admin.

Layout: bounded desktop content around 1320px, 12-column composition, 24px main gutters, 8px spacing rhythm, restrained 8–12px corner radii. These are design targets, not tested CSS. Major content stays readable at 360px. Show real hardware at useful scale; do not draw every card with a glowing gradient outline.

Theme default: system preference, explicit persistent override. A daylight theme must remain recognizably gaming through imagery, typography and structure rather than retaining dark unreadable cards. Support both languages in both themes; theme and locale are independent.

### Home page hierarchy

1. Utility/header: brand, real product/model search, market/language, theme, comparison count, account, basket.
2. Main navigation: components, assemble a PC, gaming PCs, gaming equipment, compare, buying guides/support.
3. One focused hero: the PC workbench, one primary assembly action, one component-shopping action. No auto-rotating campaign sequence.
4. Recognizable category rail: graphics cards, processors, RAM, motherboards, monitors, keyboards; a clear all-categories link reaches the rest.
5. Curated build entry points: choose a starting configuration or begin empty. Budget/resolution labels are intent filters, not invented benchmark promises.
6. Peripheral merchandising: RGB keyboards with actual form-factor/switch choices, mice/audio, chairs/desks, and setup accessories. Use a few varied image-led sections rather than dozens of identical cards.
7. Relevant product selection with model, variant, concise specs, approved price/market and availability; meaningful comparison control.
8. Build comparison entry, concise buying/assembly help, support and actual store policies.

Do not advertise free shipping, years of warranty, number of customers, countdown discounts or five-star reviews before the store has evidence for those claims.

### Product detail

Gallery with thumbnails and zoom; product title and exact part number; translated description; variants and packaging; price/market and eligibility; concise key specs; assembly/add-to-cart/compare actions; complete technical table; source-backed compatibility notes; moderated reviews; warranty/delivery information only when known. A sticky mobile buying action must not obscure specifications or validation errors.

## Assembly experience

Begin with an empty build or an explicitly described template. Browsing and experimentation require no login. Saving to an account may ask for login while preserving the draft. Users can jump between categories; do not force a long wizard on experienced builders.

Desktop layout: component navigation on the leading side, selectable parts in the middle, persistent summary on the trailing side. Mirror logical direction for Persian. The summary includes selected item count, required slots remaining, explicit compatibility findings, core-components subtotal, optional monitor/peripherals subtotal, service fee if actually offered, and estimated versus final total labels.

Mobile layout: focused category list, compact selected component rows, full-screen selection sheet, and an expandable summary above one bottom action. A second competing fixed chat/cart/summary bar is unacceptable. Keep touch targets comfortable and support keyboard navigation.

Workflow:

1. Choose platform/CPU or start from a template; explain that the choice influences motherboard/RAM options.
2. Choose motherboard, RAM, GPU, storage, case, PSU and cooling. Included coolers/integrated graphics are explicit; do not force redundant parts.
3. Optionally add monitor, keyboard, mouse, audio and setup equipment without treating them as required internal PC parts.
4. Recalculate the selected subtotal, identify incomplete sections, and explain each conflict with a fix action. Never remove the user's selection silently.
5. Save/duplicate as build A/B, compare, return to editing, then review the exact parts and transfer to cart.
6. Requote against authoritative current offers before checkout; builds are neither price locks nor stock reservations.

Compatibility has four states: compatible, incompatible, needs review, incomplete. Check board CPU support and BIOS, socket, RAM generation/slots/capacity, GPU and cooler clearance, case/board/PSU fit, required connectors and adequate power, storage slots and interfaces. Manufacturer support lists are stronger than a seller's abbreviated description. Estimated power needs a declared method and uncertainty; it is not measured consumption.

Hard conflicts, incomplete required slots and unknown core compatibility block the complete-build checkout path and show the exact reason. A user can preserve an incompatible draft while exploring alternatives or explicitly select a parts-only purchase with warnings retained; no automatic fallback is allowed. Optional equipment prices appear separately so a chair does not look like a prerequisite for a bootable PC.

The finalized implementation contracts are in the canonical roadmap, not duplicated here: each configured system keeps a group and slot identity through order history; quote validity includes exact selections and specification/rule/price revisions; virtual builds consume component stock while stocked prebuilts consume only their finished SKU. Online configuration does not promise physical assembly labor, which is disabled by default. These decisions were informed by [Shopify's grouped bundle model](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/product-merchandising/bundles/start-building) and [Adobe's customizable bundles](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/commerce-admin/catalog/products/types/product-create-bundle); Nivaro's detailed transaction policies are its own design.

## Two comparison journeys

**Two products:** same category, aligned comparable units, exact variant names, prices and availability, key differences first, all-specifications toggle, unknown values spelled out. A keyboard versus a GPU is not a useful product comparison. More than two comparison columns is not required by this brief.

**Two systems:** create A, duplicate to B, change selected parts in B, show component-by-component differences and total difference within one market/currency. Each configuration remains independently editable. Include missing/unknown components, compatibility warnings and peripheral subtotals. Do not decide a universal winner from more cores/VRAM or an invented percentage score.

Desktop comparison keeps item headings and attribute labels visible. Mobile keeps two concise columns with stacked value labels and deliberate horizontal scrolling only when unavoidable; it must not shrink a desktop table. Preserve selected builds when language/theme changes.

Measured FPS is excluded until sourced for the exact hardware, game/version, resolution, settings and upscaling/frame-generation state. A generic gaming-PC render is not evidence of how a particular component combination physically fits.

## Microinteraction specification (proposed, not implemented)

| Trigger | Intended behavior | Budget / accessibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focus/hover on actionable product | Clear boundary/action emphasis, image scale at most 1.02 | 120–180ms; keyboard gets equivalent focus indication |
| Add/replace component | Selection resolves in place, subtotal updates once, brief completion cue | 180–240ms; do not animate through false price digits |
| Open filter/component sheet | Short slide/fade and proper focus management | 180–240ms; reduced motion uses immediate state |
| Switch build A/B | Preserve scroll/context and highlight changed selection | 160–220ms; no whole-page spin or 3D fly-through |
| Toggle theme | Controlled surface transition without flash or layout shift | 120–180ms; independently readable colors |
| Enter key merchandising section | Small one-time opacity/translation | 240–320ms, only if it aids hierarchy; content visible without JS animation |
| Hero RGB detail | Optional brief, low-intensity light sweep | Stops after entry/offscreen; disabled for reduced motion; no flashing |
| Cart update/error | Immediate pending state; confirm or restore prior value with explanation | Network state is not hidden behind animation |

No scroll hijacking, magnetic cursor, parallax on essential controls, autoplay audio, endless glow loops, bouncing prices or mandatory drag interaction. The static mockups cannot demonstrate motion quality; a later explicitly authorized prototype is needed to validate it.

## Language, market and currency

The owner chose **independent international USD sales**, not conversion of the same Iranian price. The default pairs are Persian/Iran/tomans and English/international/USD, but market is a purchase decision and language is a reading preference. A language-only toggle preserves the existing basket's market. An explicit market change shows price/eligibility changes and confirms requoting before mutating the basket.

Iran stores integer rials and displays tomans. USD stores integer cents and displays dollars. A build/order/payment/refund has one market and currency. Price books are independent; no guessed exchange rate. Reports keep currency totals separate. Both markets still rely on one authoritative stock owner; two offers are not two copies of physical stock.

International checkout requires destination country and supported address/phone rules. Country eligibility, shipping, taxes/duties, seller fulfillment and the real USD provider are unresolved live-operation inputs. Static design and deterministic simulators can progress without inventing those inputs. Do not assume Zarinpal supports the international flow or that an Iranian phone/address form is universal.
